# Building the U.S. Operation

## Executive Summary

In 2019, I became the first U.S. employee of an established Australian manufacturer entering the American market. The assignment was broad: help turn a market-entry proposal into an operating U.S. business.

The work required simultaneous development of facilities, technology, inventory processes, sales support, customer support, marketing, warehousing, vendor relationships, documentation, and cross-border operating routines. I reported primarily to the Director of North America and, depending on the project, directly to the CEO.

## Business Context

The company had operated through an exclusive private-label and distribution arrangement in the United States. As that arrangement approached its end, the organization needed a confidential and practical path to establish its own U.S. presence without disrupting the existing relationship prematurely.

Before joining the payroll, I supported the early feasibility work, helped identify a viable path forward, and prepared material that contributed to board approval of the U.S. expansion proposal.

## The Challenge

At launch, the U.S. business had:

- No permanent facility
- No established U.S. technology environment
- No local inventory process
- No dedicated operating infrastructure
- A very small initial team
- Significant product inventory arriving from Australia
- New sales representatives scheduled to join
- A need to coordinate frequently with systems, finance, operations, and leadership in Australia

The business needed to appear credible and structured externally while much of the operating foundation was still being built internally.

## My Role

My responsibility was effectively to address whatever was required to create the U.S. branch.

That included:

- Supporting the market-entry and feasibility process
- Securing a temporary operating location
- Coordinating the permanent facility project
- Establishing technology and communications systems
- Configuring inventory, pricing, purchasing, invoicing, and sales-order workflows
- Supporting sales infrastructure and lead planning
- Organizing product storage and warehouse operations
- Building SOPs and operating documentation
- Training and onboarding employees
- Coordinating with Australian leadership and system specialists
- Leading trade-show, marketing, and sales-support initiatives

## Phase 1: Confidential Feasibility and Market Entry

The project began before my formal employment. Because the existing distribution agreement was still active, early planning required careful confidentiality and compartmentalization.

I developed and supported a feasibility proposal, identified potential service partners, and helped define a practical route into the market. The turning point came when the company’s board approved the proposal.

## Phase 2: Temporary U.S. Operations Center

I secured an interim location in Cleveland, Tennessee, using a month-to-month office and storage facility.

Approximately one month into the launch, senior leaders and a systems specialist joined me to help receive and organize roughly $5 million in initial inventory. Together, we established temporary storage, office space, and the basic operating environment needed to begin conducting business.

I then prepared technology, accounts, computers, cellular service, and working access for incoming sales representatives.

## Phase 3: Technology and Business Systems Launch

Working with the Australian systems team, I helped establish U.S. instances and workflows for core systems, including:

- Virtual office communications
- Hosted QuickBooks Desktop
- CRM access and data structures
- Shared file systems
- Role-based permissions
- Backup routines
- Employee equipment and account provisioning

Within QuickBooks, I configured the inventory environment, loaded product data, established purchase and sales pricing, created multiple pricing levels, and supported workflows covering sales orders, invoicing, payment, and purchase orders to Australia.

The CRM was adapted for field-sales use, including mobile access, voice-dictated notes, and additional fields for customer characteristics, annual pool volume, equipment preferences, and compatibility requirements.

## Phase 4: Permanent Facility Development

The permanent site was a 5,000-square-foot warehouse and office facility in Ringgold, Georgia, including approximately 900 square feet of office space.

I helped design the layout, guided and negotiated the build-out, monitored site preparation, coordinated subcontractors, procured furniture and appliances, arranged finish work, and designed the warehouse storage plan.

When one subcontractor’s work failed to meet expectations, my documentation clearly established the performance deficiencies. The company was able to end the relationship, limit exposure, and replace the contractor.

## Phase 5: Inventory Transition and Operational Launch

The move from the temporary site to the permanent facility took place over approximately one week.

With one temporary worker, I repeatedly loaded a 26-foot box truck, transported inventory from Cleveland to Ringgold, and organized the products in the new warehouse. This occurred while I continued coordinating remaining facility work and preparing the operation for launch.

The permanent facility opened around mid-February 2020.

## Phase 6: Sales and Market Support

The early sales team worked leads sourced from warranty data, purchased lists, and direct prospecting. I supplemented this with data mining and weekly call-cycle planning.

I also helped coordinate an external manufacturer’s representative group, supported trade-show preparation, developed sales tools, trained employees on product features and technical troubleshooting, and led marketing initiatives across trade shows, digital campaigns, and email promotions.

## Operating Model Established

The resulting U.S. operating foundation included:

- A functioning temporary launch site
- A completed permanent headquarters and warehouse
- Core technology and communications systems
- Inventory, pricing, purchasing, sales-order, and invoicing workflows
- CRM and lead-management infrastructure
- Role-based shared files and backups
- Employee onboarding and technical training
- Warehouse organization and storage processes
- Sales-support and market-development routines
- Cross-border coordination with Australia

## Leadership Lessons

### Build credibility while the infrastructure is still developing

A new operation must look dependable to customers and partners before every internal system is mature. Professional communications, reliable follow-through, and operational redundancy were critical in the early months.

### Stay close to execution

This project required executive coordination and physical execution: business cases, contractor negotiations, server access, system setup, product movement, warehouse design, employee training, and customer support all formed part of the same launch.

### Documentation protects the organization

Detailed documentation was essential when contractor performance became unacceptable. It converted a subjective disagreement into a clear, defensible operating decision.

### Systems create scale

The facility alone did not create the business. The operating system around it—technology, workflows, documentation, permissions, training, forecasting, and communication—made the location usable and repeatable.
